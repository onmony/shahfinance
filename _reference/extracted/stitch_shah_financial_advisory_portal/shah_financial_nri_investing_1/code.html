Clarity First

The 5 critical questions every NRI asks before investing

Direct, practical answers with zero financial jargon.

01

NRE vs NRO: Which account should I use for mutual funds?

keyboard_arrow_down

NRE (Non-Resident External) funds are fully repatriable back overseas in foreign currency with zero tax in India on interest earned. Mutual funds purchased through an NRE account redeem directly into your NRE account and can be remitted overseas at any time.

NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) accounts are designed for income generated in India (rental income from Hyderabad properties, dividends, pensions). Funds in NRO can also be invested in mutual funds, but repatriation requires Form 15CA/15CB documentation up to USD 1 Million per fiscal year.

Read the comprehensive NRE/NRO guide arrow_forward
02

Tax and TDS: How are capital gains taxed for NRIs in India?

keyboard_arrow_down

Unlike resident Indians where tax is settled at filing, mutual fund houses in India are mandated by law to deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on all NRI redemptions at applicable rates (e.g., standard short-term or long-term capital gains rates plus surcharge and cess).

However, because India holds bilateral Double Tax Avoidance Agreements (DTAA) with over 85 nations (including the US, UK, UAE, Singapore, and Australia), you can generally claim the Indian TDS as a Foreign Tax Credit against your resident country tax liability, eliminating duplicate tax.

See DTAA computation matrix arrow_forward
03

Repatriation: Can I take my money back out of India easily?

keyboard_arrow_down

Yes, completely. Capital invested via an NRE account along with all accrued profits is 100% freely repatriable with zero currency limit and minimal procedural friction directly through your bank's net banking portal.

If your investments originate from an NRO account, the Reserve Bank of India allows outward repatriation of up to USD 1,000,000 per financial year under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) framework with a standard Chartered Accountant certificate (Form 15CB) and tax declaration (Form 15CA), which our team assists in coordinating.

Step-by-step repatriation procedure arrow_forward
04

Country Restrictions: Can US or Canadian NRIs invest in Indian mutual funds?

keyboard_arrow_down

Yes, but with strict AMC curation. Because of stringent Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) and Common Reporting Standard (CRS) regulatory burdens, several Indian asset management companies have stopped accepting investments from US and Canada residents.

However, prominent institutions (such as select major private fund houses) continue to welcome US and Canadian investors subject to specific declarations. We maintain a verified, active roster of compliant AMCs so you never invest into schemes that reject your FATCA documentation.

05

Remote Onboarding: Do I need to fly to Hyderabad to open accounts?

keyboard_arrow_down

Not at all. Over 95% of our overseas clients have never set foot inside our Begumpet office. The entire process—including AMFI/SEBI KYC registration, In-Person Verification (IPV via secure video call), foreign address verification, and digital mandates—is executed entirely through your smartphone or laptop.

You only require an active passport, PAN card, foreign address proof (utility bill/bank statement), and an Indian NRE or NRO bank account. We facilitate everything end-to-end remotely.

Knowledge Base

Frequently asked questions by overseas Indians

Everything you need to know about FEMA rules, taxation, and remote account setup.

1. Can NRIs legally invest in Indian mutual funds? keyboard_arrow_down
Yes, under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) and SEBI guidelines, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and Persons of Indian Origin (PIOs) / Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs) are fully permitted to invest in Indian mutual funds on a repatriable (via NRE account) or non-repatriable (via NRO account) basis.
2. NRE vs NRO vs FCNR accounts: which should I use for mutual funds? keyboard_arrow_down
Mutual funds cannot be directly debited from foreign-currency FCNR deposits. You must use either an NRE (Non-Resident External) or NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) INR bank account. If you remit foreign currency from your overseas salary, an NRE account ensures full, unhindered repatriation of redemption proceeds back overseas. If your source of investment is local Indian income (rents, dividends), use an NRO account.
3. What are the specific rules for NRIs residing in the USA and Canada? keyboard_arrow_down
Due to FATCA reporting and US SEC / Canadian regulatory compliance, certain Indian mutual fund houses restrict or disallow investments from US/Canada NRIs. However, several leading AMCs do allow investments through paperless FATCA declarations. We guide US/Canada investors exclusively to compliant AMCs and advise on matching annual capital gain statements to assist your US CPA with IRS Form 8621 (PFIC) filings.
4. How does TDS work for NRIs and how can I claim tax credits abroad? keyboard_arrow_down
Mutual funds automatically deduct TDS on capital gains before disbursing redemption proceeds to your NRE/NRO account. You will receive a Form 16A quarterly from the fund house. Under DTAA provisions, you can present this Indian TDS certificate in your country of tax residency to claim a credit or tax offset against local capital gains taxes, avoiding double taxation.
5. What is the step-by-step repatriation procedure under FEMA? keyboard_arrow_down
For NRE-linked investments, redemption proceeds land back into your NRE account and can be repatriated overseas with zero documentation or RBI approvals via standard net-banking wire. For NRO accounts, you are allowed up to USD 1,000,000 per financial year by submitting Form 15CA (online tax undertaking) and Form 15CB (certified by an Indian Chartered Accountant confirming taxes are paid).
6. How does remote Aadhaar/Passport KYC work without physical presence in India? keyboard_arrow_down
AMFI allows digital KYC via secure video verification (In-Person Verification or IPV). You upload your Indian PAN card, foreign address proof (such as an overseas bank statement or driving license), and a copy of your Indian passport. During a 3-minute video verification link on your phone, you show your original documents. Your KRA status updates digitally within 48 to 72 hours.
7. Can you help coordinate my parents' living and medical expenses in Hyderabad? keyboard_arrow_down
Yes. This is one of our most requested advisory workflows. We can construct an NRO-linked portfolio with automated Systematic Withdrawal Plans (SWP) that deposit steady monthly income into your parents' local Hyderabad bank accounts, shielding them from market swings and freeing you from constant manual remittances.
8. What should I do with my Indian investments when I plan to return permanently to India? keyboard_arrow_down
When you return to India for permanent settlement, your status shifts from NRI to RNOR (Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident) for roughly 2 to 3 years. You must notify your bank to re-designate NRE accounts to RFC (Resident Foreign Currency) or resident savings accounts. Your existing mutual fund folios can seamlessly continue with a simple status change form, without liquidating or triggering capital gains taxes.
